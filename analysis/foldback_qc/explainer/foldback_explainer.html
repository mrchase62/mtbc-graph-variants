How fold-back chimeras make false SV calls

A fold-back chimera is a single library molecule that reads along the genome and then turns back on itself, continuing along the opposite strand. Aligned, it looks exactly like a read crossing an inversion breakpoint. When enough of these reads pile up at one spot, SV callers report a small inversion that is not in the genome.

What the read-removal experiment showed. In 9 chimeric libraries mapped to their own finished genomes, where every SV call is false, deleting only the fold-back read pairs (1.3–10.7% of pairs) removed 95% of our caller's false calls and 90% of dysgu's. Deleting the same number of random pairs removed none.

1

The molecule folds back during library prep

Fragmenting DNA (sonication, nuclease or Tn5) leaves ends with single-stranded stretches. If that stretch contains an inverted repeat, a short sequence followed nearby by its reverse complement, the end can pair with itself. A polymerase in end repair or gap filling then copies back along the template, joining the forward strand to the reverse complement of nearby sequence. This is the mechanism Haile et al. (2019) described for sonicated and enzymatically fragmented libraries.

2

The aligner splits the read across both strands

bwa aligns the first part of the read on one strand and the second part on the other strand a short distance away. The second part is recorded as a supplementary alignment (the SA tag), or, when it is too short to place, as a soft clip. Below is a real read from isolate N1176, a 151 bp Tn5 library.

The distance between the two pieces depends on the library. In most of these libraries the second piece lands back on top of the first (a tight hairpin, pieces overlapping). In N1176, the worst library, it usually lands 100–600 bp away (a wider loop).

3

Callers read the split as an inversion

An SV caller collects reads whose pieces join opposite strands, clusters them by position, and calls an event once enough reads agree. Opposite-strand joins within a few hundred bases are the signature of a small inversion, so that is what comes out. The size reported is roughly the distance between the two pieces.

The difference from a real inversion is in what the other reads at the site show. At a real inversion every read that spans the breakpoint carries the join, there is a matching second breakpoint, and read depth is normal. At a fold-back junction only a few reads carry the join and every other read shows the reference.

The calls that disappeared when the fold-back reads were removed are almost all inversions (or our caller's unpaired breakends, which are the same signal without a partner). Nearly all are 50–500 bp.

4

Recurrence turns noise into a call

A single fold-back read is ignored: callers need several reads supporting the same junction. Two things stack reads at one junction.

PCR copies. One chimeric molecule amplified during library PCR gives several identical reads. P2 does not mark duplicates, so each copy counts as support. In N0004 only 42% of reads at junctions with 5 or more reads were distinct molecules.

Sequence hotspots. Even after collapsing copies, many junctions hold 5 or more independent molecules. Those sites are enriched for inverted repeats, the sequence that lets the end fold back in step 1.

5

Why an allele-fraction floor is not enough

The allele fraction a caller reports is the share of reads at the site carrying the join. At a hotspot that can easily reach 10–20%, and dysgu, which counts reads differently, sometimes reports 50% or more. A 10% floor removes about half of our caller's fold-back calls; a 20% floor removes most of ours but leaves 1,703 dysgu calls in these 9 libraries.

6

Removing the fold-back reads removes the calls

Each library was run four ways: all reads; half of the fold-back pairs removed; all of them removed; and the same number of random non-fold-back pairs removed, which controls for the loss of depth. Insert size, library, genome and everything else stayed the same.

What this means for the pipeline

Small inversions and unpaired breakends from short reads are the calls to distrust; the agreed robust tier already leaves them out. Sample QC should catch libraries with many recurrent junctions, which the fold-back rate tracks well and the count of recurrent junctions may track better. The selfcal54 isolates will test where the false calls start, and whether insert size or junction recurrence adds to the rate.
